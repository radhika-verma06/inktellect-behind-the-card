01 / Capture

One line, recorded as motion.

A phone canvas records ordered x, y and pen-up/down. Not a photo of handwriting - the actual path the pen took, in the order it took it.

The line becomes a list of small movements: draw, lift, move, draw. That is what a robot needs; a picture of the word is not enough.

02 / Model

A small machine that predicts the next pen movement.

The deployed model is a pretrained Graves LSTM with attention and a mixture-density head. It does not draw letters from a font. At every step it predicts where the pen goes next.

-> ->
-> -> feeds the decoder
Attention window

It learns where to look.

A soft window slides over the requested text and focuses on the next character. The decoder never sees the whole sentence at once; it learns where to look, one character at a time.

The model was pretrained on IAM-OnDB. The personal fine-tune uses 12 short prompts; exact replay bypasses the model and uses the captured strokes directly.

03 / Two ways

Replay the line, or continue the hand.

The card on the front is the exact replay path. The other path is the one that turns one line into a page.

The line on the card is yours.

The robot writes the visitor's captured strokes, in the same order and with the same pen lifts. No generation, no font, no guessing which word came from where.

What you seeYour own line, written again by the arm. The card is a physical copy of the motion you made.

New words, same hand.

The model reads the captured hand as a style signal and writes new text from it. This is the "one line in, a page out" path. It is generative: the shape can vary, and it is not a perfect clone.

What you seeA longer line or page in a hand close to the captured one. More words, more variation, and an honest few-shot result.
04 / Arm

Real pen. Real paper. Real ink.

The capture becomes a trajectory. The trajectory becomes a set of XY points with a pen-up or pen-down state. The arm is a Dobot Magician, calibrated to the paper and the pen.
