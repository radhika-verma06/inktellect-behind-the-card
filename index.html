The reason behind the card

Technology should help our care reach further.

When someone you love is far away, the words matter. So does the way they arrive. A font is useful, and a text message is immediate, but neither carries the little turns and pauses that make a handwritten note feel familiar.

I am not trying to replace people writing to each other. I want to make it easier to send something personal when distance, time, or the effort of writing gets in the way. A robot can hold the pen; the message, the intention, and the handwriting sample still come from a person.

That is the idea behind InkTellect: give the system a small record of a person's pen movement, then explore whether it can help put new words on paper in a hand that feels closer to theirs.

If a machine helps a letter feel more like it came from you, it is carrying your voice - not taking its place.
Where the prototype is today: it can replay captured pen strokes exactly, or generate new text with a personal fine-tune. That fine-tune used 12 short prompts. A photo alone does not reveal stroke order or pen lifts, and this is still an experiment - not a perfect handwriting copy.

01 / Capture

One line, recorded as motion.

A phone canvas records ordered x, y and pen-up/down. Not a photo of handwriting - the actual path the pen took, in the order it took it.

The line becomes a list of small movements: draw, lift, move, draw. That is what a robot needs; a picture of the word is not enough.

02 / Technical

A small machine that predicts the next pen movement.

The generation path uses a pretrained Graves LSTM with an attention window and a mixture-density output. It does not paste letter shapes from a font: it predicts a stream of small pen movements. Exact replay is a separate route that sends captured strokes straight to the path planner.

Why capture motion instead of only a photo? A still image shows the final ink, but not the order of strokes or where the pen lifted. The current capture records that movement. The exact-replay path bypasses generation; the personal fine-tune path uses captured examples to adapt a pretrained model.

Attention window

It learns where to look.

A soft window slides over the requested text and focuses on the next character. The decoder never sees the whole sentence at once; it learns where to look, one character at a time.

The model was pretrained on IAM-OnDB. The personal fine-tune uses 12 short prompts; exact replay bypasses the model and uses the captured strokes directly.

03 / Two ways

Replay the line, or continue the hand.

The card on the front shows exact replay. The generative path is different: a personal fine-tune uses a small captured dataset to help the model write new text. It can vary, and it is not a perfect clone.
